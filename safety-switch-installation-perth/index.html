Questions Answered

Safety Switch FAQ

A safety switch (RCD) detects electrical faults and cuts power in milliseconds, preventing electrocution and electrical fires. It's one of the most important safety devices in your home. AS/NZS 3000 requires full RCD protection on all new installations and major upgrades.
AS/NZS 3000 requires all final subcircuits to be RCD protected in new installations and upgrades. For existing homes, there's no blanket legal requirement, but we strongly recommend it for safety. If you rent: annual professional testing is required under WA tenancy regulations.
Rental properties: annual professional testing required by law. Owner-occupied homes: every 2 years as best practice (not legally required, but highly recommended). We provide test certificates for every inspection, which is essential for rental compliance.
Usually yes. We can retrofit RCDs to most existing boards without full replacement. Some older switchboards may need replacement if they're incompatible or unsafe. We assess your specific board and recommend the most cost-effective solution.
Constant tripping means your RCD is doing its job: detecting a fault in the circuit. Never bypass it. Causes include: damaged cable insulation, faulty appliance, water ingress, or poor wiring. We locate the fault and fix the underlying problem, not just the symptom.
Circuit-by-circuit RCDs are preferable. A switchboard RCD cuts power to your entire house if it trips, leaving you without lighting or heating. Individual circuit RCDs protect only that circuit, so other circuits keep working. Ask us which approach suits your property.
