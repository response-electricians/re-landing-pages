Three-phase power Perth: when you need it, what’s involved, how long it takes

If you’ve added an EV charger, a ducted reverse-cycle aircon, a pool pump and a workshop over the last few years, you’ve quietly outgrown single-phase power. Lights dim when the aircon starts. The EV charger throttles when the oven kicks on. Every big load fights every other big load for supply. Here’s what three-phase actually is, the four signs you need it, and what the upgrade involves.

What three-phase power actually is (in plain English)

Forget the engineering. Here’s the analogy:

Single-phase is a one-lane road. One wire brings power from the pole into your home. Every appliance draws off the same line. When the aircon compressor kicks on, everything else has to wait its turn. Lights flicker. The EV charger throttles. The pool pump lags. Voltage sags across the whole house whenever a big load starts.

Three-phase is a three-lane road. Three separate wires bring power in from the pole. The voltage per phase is still 230-240V, but there are three of them running in parallel. Appliances get spread across the three phases so no single line carries everything. Big loads run without stealing supply from small loads. The house behaves like a bigger system, because it is one.

The upgrade doesn’t change what your appliances are or how they behave individually. It changes the underlying supply, so everything runs comfortably at the same time.

Four signs your home has outgrown single-phase

The tipping point isn’t about any single appliance. It’s about the simultaneous load your home wants to draw. If two or more of these are true, three-phase is worth the conversation:

1. You’re running an EV charger and ducted aircon

Charging an EV overnight while running ducted aircon on a hot day is the number-one giveaway. Some newer chargers have load-management features that throttle the charger when the aircon starts — that’s the software papering over the hardware problem. Three-phase gives each of them their own supply lane.

2. Your pool pump is on its own circuit

Pool pumps draw serious startup current, especially variable-speed models kicking to full speed. Add a heat pump for the pool and single-phase is really stretched. Three-phase pool systems run more efficiently and don’t take the rest of the house down with them at startup.

3. You have a workshop with real machinery

Larger table saws, welders, air compressors, dust extractors and lathes are designed for three-phase. On single-phase you’re running smaller motors, using slower cycles, or getting single-phase converter workarounds. If the workshop matters, three-phase unlocks the tools you actually want.

4. Lights flicker when big loads start

The classic symptom. Lights dim when the aircon compressor engages, when the oven element kicks on, when the pool pump primes. That’s single-phase voltage dropping across the whole house because one big load has grabbed most of the supply. Three-phase holds voltage cleanly because the load is spread.

What actually happens during a three-phase upgrade

Three-phase isn’t a rewire of your house. It’s an upgrade of the supply infrastructure that feeds your existing wiring. Here’s the scope:

  • New consumer mains — the cables that run from your meter box to your main switchboard. Single-phase mains have three conductors (active, neutral, earth). Three-phase mains have five (three actives, neutral, earth). Bigger conductors, more current capacity.
  • Meter box changes — the meter and the meter box get updated to handle three-phase. Western Power coordinates the meter change as part of the application.
  • New main switchboard — a three-phase switchboard is bigger than a single-phase one. Three-column layout, phase-coloured bus bars (red, white, blue in older WA installs; or brown/black/grey in newer TN-C-S installs), space for balanced circuit distribution.
  • Circuit rebalancing — your existing circuits get redistributed across the three phases so the load is balanced. Heavy loads (aircon, oven, hot water, EV charger) get dedicated phase assignments so they don’t stack on one line.
  • Optional dedicated three-phase circuits — if you have three-phase-native gear (bigger EV charger, workshop equipment, commercial aircon), those get dedicated three-phase circuits at the same time.
  • Certification — Notice of Completion, Electrical Safety Certificate. Filed with EnergySafety WA.

Your internal wiring downstream of the switchboard stays as-is unless you specifically want major appliances re-routed onto new dedicated circuits.

The Western Power application (why it takes weeks)

You can’t just upgrade to three-phase on a whim. Western Power owns the supply network up to your meter, and they have to approve any change in supply type. Here’s what’s involved:

1

Load assessment

We measure your current maximum demand, calculate your projected three-phase demand, and document the load balancing plan. Bigger jobs need a detailed engineering submission; residential upgrades are simpler.

2

Application submitted

We lodge the connection application with Western Power on your behalf. Includes site details, load calculations, proposed switchboard specs, meter change requirements.

3

Western Power review & approval

Typically 4 to 8 weeks. They’re checking that the local network can supply the new demand, that no upgrade work is needed upstream (transformer, pole, street cable), and scheduling the meter change.

4

Install date scheduled

Once approved, we book the physical install — typically one day (occasionally two for larger jobs). Power off for 4-6 hours while we swap the consumer mains and the switchboard. Meter change happens the same day or shortly after.

5

Certified & live

Notice of Completion issued, Electrical Safety Certificate on the day. Three-phase supply live. Any dedicated three-phase circuits (EV charger, workshop) commissioned at the same time.

The bottom line: book now to install before the next summer peak. The Western Power lead time is the constraint, and it doesn’t get shorter by waiting.

Commercial and business premises

Most commercial premises above the smallest scale need three-phase. Commercial aircon, refrigeration, workshop machinery, kitchen equipment, EV fleet chargers and lifts are all designed for three-phase supply. For a commercial site upgrading to accommodate new equipment (or a new tenant), the three-phase question comes up early.

Two common commercial scenarios:

  • New tenant fitout — the incoming tenant needs three-phase for their equipment. Fastest path is to upgrade the supply before the fitout starts, not during it.
  • Expanding operations — existing single-phase business adds equipment (bigger compressor, more refrigeration, EV fleet charging) and hits the supply ceiling. Three-phase upgrade unlocks the growth.

Commercial three-phase upgrades typically need more coordination — scheduling around trading hours, coordinating with adjacent tenants if the supply is shared, working around commercial insurance and access. We handle all of that.

Common questions about three-phase power in Perth

What’s the actual difference between single-phase and three-phase power?+

Think of it as one lane versus three lanes on the same road. Single-phase brings one live conductor from the street into your home — every appliance draws off the same supply line. Three-phase brings three separate live conductors, each carrying its own load. The voltage per phase is the same (230-240V), but the total capacity is doubled or tripled, and appliances can be balanced across the three phases so no single line gets overloaded. That’s why lights don’t flicker when the aircon starts on three-phase — the aircon is on a different phase from the lighting.

Do I need three-phase for just a single EV charger?+

Not usually. Most home EV chargers run happily on single-phase up to 7.4 kW (32A). Three-phase becomes worth it when you want faster charging (11-22 kW three-phase chargers), when you have two EVs, or — more commonly — when the EV charger is one of several major loads competing for supply (ducted aircon, pool, workshop, hot water heat pump). The tipping point isn’t any single appliance — it’s the total simultaneous load your home wants to draw.

How long does the whole three-phase upgrade take from booking to install?+

The bulk of the wait is the Western Power application — typically 4 to 8 weeks from lodgement, sometimes longer at peak times. We can’t do the physical work until that application is approved. Once approved, the on-site work is usually one day (occasionally two for larger jobs). Book now and the whole upgrade lands well before the next summer’s peak load hits.

Can I upgrade to three-phase without rewiring the whole house?+

Yes, in most cases. The upgrade replaces the consumer mains (the cables from the meter to the switchboard), the switchboard itself, and the meter box wiring where required. Your existing internal wiring generally stays as-is unless you want specific major appliances (EV charger, ducted aircon, workshop) re-routed onto dedicated three-phase circuits. Full rewires happen only if your existing wiring is at end-of-life anyway — separate conversation.

Will three-phase reduce my electricity bill?+

Slightly, in specific cases. Three-phase motors are more efficient than single-phase equivalents, so pool pumps, larger aircon compressors and workshop tools run a bit cheaper on three-phase. The bigger benefit is avoiding voltage-drop losses when big loads run — on single-phase, running everything at once means lower voltage at every appliance, which shortens equipment life and wastes energy. Three-phase holds voltage cleanly. Real savings come from the equipment lasting longer, not from meter readings.

Is three-phase required for commercial premises?+

Most commercial premises above the smallest scale need three-phase. Commercial aircon, refrigeration, workshop machinery, kitchen equipment and lifts are all designed for three-phase supply. For a commercial site upgrading to accommodate new equipment (or a new tenant), the three-phase question comes up early. Book the assessment before you buy the equipment — supply capacity determines what you can install.

What happens on the day of install — will my power be off?+

Yes, for most of the working day. We isolate the supply at the pole while we replace the consumer mains and the switchboard. Typical outage window is 4 to 6 hours on install day. We give you at least a week’s notice so you can plan around it — freezers full, work-from-home arrangements, fridge contents. Power is back on before we leave, with everything tested and certified.
