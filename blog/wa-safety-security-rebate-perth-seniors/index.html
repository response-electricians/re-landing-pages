The WA Safety and Security Rebate: up to $400 back for Perth seniors

If you hold a WA Seniors Card, the Department of Communities runs a Safety and Security Rebate that gives you up to $400 back on eligible home safety and security work. Six of the items on the list are things we install as licensed electricians and security installers — safety switches, mains-powered smoke alarms, sensor lights, video doorbells, home alarms and CCTV. Here’s what the scheme covers, who qualifies, and how to claim it.

What the rebate is, in plain English

The WA Safety and Security Rebate is a Department of Communities scheme run through the WA Seniors Card program. It gives eligible Seniors Card holders up to $400 per household towards the cost of eligible home safety and security items. The scheme was reintroduced on 22 August 2021, and only purchases made on or after that date can be claimed.

Important framing: the $400 is a household cap for the life of the scheme, not a per-year benefit and not a per-item benefit. Partners share the household allocation. You can spread the $400 across one or more eligible items, but once claimed, no further claim can be made from the same household.

Sources: WA.gov.au — Apply for a Safety and Security Rebate and Seniors Card WA Terms and Conditions.

Already claimed under the old scheme?

If you received a rebate under the previous 2009–2015 scheme, you’re still eligible to apply under this current one. The two are treated separately. Terms § 7.

Who is eligible

To claim the rebate, an applicant must:

  1. Hold a valid WA Seniors Card at the time of purchase. This is the key eligibility gate.
  2. Reside in an "eligible household" in Western Australia as their principal place of residence.

WA Seniors Card eligibility, in short

To hold a WA Seniors Card, you must be aged 65 or above, an Australian citizen or permanent resident, residing in Western Australia, and either permanently retired or working no more than 25 hours per week in paid employment.

Source: Seniors Card WA — Eligibility.

Eligible household types

The rebate rules recognise a wide range of living situations. All of the following count:

  • Owner-occupied home (fully or partly owned)
  • Single dwelling where you don’t own or rent but live with family
  • Rental (including Housing Authority tenancies) — with landlord/strata approval
  • Granny flat forming part of a family residence
  • Retirement village (independent living unit, serviced apartment, hostel arrangement)
  • Caravan or mobile home used as your principal place of residence

Not eligible: Residents living in aged care organisations, hospitals, hostels, or religious order accommodation.

Source: Terms and Conditions §§ 4–6.

The six items on the list that we install

The full rebate list includes eleven categories (electrical items, security items, fire equipment, and repairs). Six of them sit inside our licensed scope as an Electrical Contractor and Security Agent — here’s the summary, with the maximum rebate for each straight from the WA Seniors Card terms.

A few things worth knowing item-by-item

  • Safety switch (RCD): The RCD sits at the full $400 cap. WA law requires it to be installed by a licensed Electrical Contractor per the Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991. Must comply with AS/NZS 3000-2018 to be eligible.
  • Mains-powered smoke alarm: $200 rebate per alarm, minimum purchase and installation value of $100. Must comply with AS 3786-2014. Multiple alarms can be claimed toward the household $400 cap.
  • Security sensor light: $200 rebate per light, minimum $50. Licensed electrician required if the light is connected to mains power. Battery or solar-only sensor lights can be DIY.
  • Video doorbell: $200 rebate per doorbell, minimum $100. Ongoing subscription fees are not eligible — only the hardware and install cost. Licensed electrician required if mains-powered.
  • Home alarm and CCTV: Combined category, up to $400 per household. Minimum $200 purchase. Contractor must hold both a Security Agent licence and a Security Installer licence.
The rebate list also covers items we don’t install: security door screens, deadlocks, security window screens/shutters, fire extinguishers and fire blankets. These are all eligible under the scheme — you’ll want a specialist screen fitter, locksmith, or fire safety supplier for those. Full item list at seniorscard.wa.gov.au.

Why we’re a fit for the electrical AND security items

Most electrical companies in Perth can install the RCD, smoke alarm, sensor light and video doorbell items on the list — that’s standard electrical work under an Electrical Contractor licence. Fewer are also licensed to install home alarm systems and CCTV, because that’s a separate licensing regime.

Home alarms and CCTV in WA require both a Security Agent licence (for the business) and a Security Installer licence (for the individual technician), issued by WA Police under the Security and Related Activities (Control) Regulations 1997. Response Electricians holds both, alongside our Electrical Contractor licence EC009022.

Practical outcome for you: one licensed team can install everything on the "electrical" side of the rebate list AND everything on the "security" side that we’re licensed for — on one visit, one invoice, one tax receipt. That receipt is what the WA Seniors Card Centre needs to process your claim.

How to claim the rebate

Apply directly on the official website

All claims go through the WA Government — not through us. You can apply online, download the form, or ring the WA Seniors Card Centre. Full details on the official page: wa.gov.au — Apply for a Safety and Security Rebate →

1

Confirm your Seniors Card is current

You must hold a valid WA Seniors Card at the time of purchase. If you’re not a member yet, apply at seniorscard.wa.gov.au before booking any work. Your partner’s card counts for the household — but only one claim per household total.

2

Book, install, pay in full

Get the eligible items installed by a properly licensed contractor. Pay the invoice in full. Keep the tax receipt — it must show the supplier’s ABN and date to be valid for the claim.

3

Apply to the WA Seniors Card Centre

Apply online through your WA Seniors Card account at seniorscard.wa.gov.au, download and post/email a form, call 1800 671 233, or visit the Centre at 140 William Street, Perth (or any Department of Communities customer service counter).

4

Rebate is paid by EFT

Once your claim is approved, the rebate is paid by electronic funds transfer into your nominated account. Payments are only made to accounts held in the applicant’s name (with limited exceptions for partners with a WA Seniors Card, Power of Attorney, or a court-appointed Guardian).

Two rebates, not one

You can apply for the state Safety and Security Rebate AND a similar rebate through your local council if they run one. Perth-area councils including City of South Perth, City of Vincent, and City of Canning offer their own separate programs with different eligibility and amounts — worth checking your council’s website. Terms § 13.

If you’re unsure, check before you buy

Eligibility for the rebate is set by the Department of Communities, not by us — and the scheme rules can change at their discretion. If you have any doubt about whether an item, an install, or a household situation qualifies, we recommend calling the WA Seniors Card Centre on 1800 671 233 before you book the work. They can confirm eligibility on the phone and save you a wasted claim.

We’re happy to give you a fixed price on the phone for any of the six items we install, so you know what the tax receipt will look like before you commit — and you can ring the Seniors Card Centre to check the numbers add up against your remaining household cap.

Common questions about the WA Safety and Security Rebate

Who is eligible for the WA Safety and Security Rebate?+

You must hold a valid WA Seniors Card at the time of purchase and live in an eligible household in Western Australia as your principal place of residence. Owner-occupier, single dwelling, rental (with landlord/strata approval), granny flat, retirement village, and caravan/mobile home used as principal residence all count. Aged care, hospital, hostel and religious order accommodation is not eligible.

How much can I claim?+

Up to $400 per household as a one-time claim under the current scheme. If your eligible purchase is less than $400, you receive up to the amount you spent. Applicants who received a rebate under the previous 2009–2015 scheme are eligible to apply again.

What items qualify for the rebate?+

Home alarm and CCTV systems, security door screens, deadlocks, security window screens or shutters, security sensor lights, Wi-Fi or wired video doorbells, safety switches (RCDs), mains-powered smoke alarms, fire extinguishers, fire blankets, and repairs of any of these. Response Electricians is licensed to install six of them — safety switches, mains smoke alarms, sensor lights, video doorbells, home alarms and CCTV.

Can I claim more than once?+

No. One claim per household under the current scheme, up to $400 total. Partners share the household allocation. If you claim less than $400, you can’t make a further claim for the balance.

Do the electrical items have to be installed by a licensed contractor?+

Yes. WA law requires electrical work — including safety switches, mains-powered smoke alarms, and any mains-powered sensor light or video doorbell — to be installed by a licensed Electrical Contractor. Response Electricians is EC009022. Home alarms and CCTV also require a Security Agent and Installer licence, which we hold.

Can I claim if I’m renting?+

Yes. Renters holding a WA Seniors Card can claim. You must first get approval from the landlord or strata company to install the eligible item — that’s your responsibility, not the electrician’s.

Can I combine this with a local council rebate?+

Yes. The WA Seniors Card Terms and Conditions confirm that applicants can apply for a rebate under this scheme AND similar rebates through their local council. Amounts and eligibility differ by council — check your council’s website.

How do I apply?+

Buy, install and pay in full first. Then apply online through your WA Seniors Card account, by downloadable form (post or email), by phone on 1800 671 233, or in person at 140 William Street, Perth or any Department of Communities customer service counter. Include your tax receipt with the supplier ABN.
