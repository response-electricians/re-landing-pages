EOFY electrical Perth: 4 weeks left to book before 30 June

EOFY isn’t just for businesses. Investment property owners, sole traders, and households running a small business from a home office can all claim electrical work against this financial year — if it’s installed before 30 June. Four weeks left. Here’s what typically qualifies, who books it most, and how to lock it in before EOFY closes.

What the May 2026 Budget changed

The big EOFY headline this year is the $20,000 instant asset write-off — made permanent in the 12 May 2026 Federal Budget. Eligible businesses can deduct the full cost of an asset under $20,000 in the year it’s first used or installed ready for use. No more annual extensions, no more uncertainty. That’s most relevant to commercial customers, but sole traders running a registered business are also eligible.

For private homeowners, EOFY still matters for two reasons that don’t depend on the Budget:

  • Investment properties. Electrical work on a rental property is generally tax-deductible — either immediately (as a repair) or over time (as a capital works deduction or plant and equipment depreciation), depending on the nature of the work.
  • Home offices and sole traders. If you run a small business from home, the business-use portion of electrical work that supports your work area is typically claimable. Dedicated power circuits, lighting upgrades, EV chargers used for business travel — all candidates.

This guide covers what those rules generally mean, what jobs typically qualify, who books them most, and the bit that catches people out — the installed-and-operational-by-30-June rule.

Investment property: how electrical work is typically claimed

Electrical work on a rental property generally falls into one of three categories. Each is claimed differently, and that’s the conversation to have with your accountant before you book.

Repairs and maintenance

Like-for-like replacement of something that’s broken — replacing a failed RCD, swapping a faulty power point, repairing a fault on an existing circuit. Typically deductible in the year you pay for it.

Capital works

Improvements that extend the life of the property or upgrade something to a higher standard — a full switchboard upgrade from old ceramic fuses to modern Clipsal Resi MAX, a rewire, adding new circuits. Typically deducted over time as a capital works deduction.

Plant and equipment

Discrete items installed in the property — an EV charger, smoke alarms, exhaust fans, smart-home gear. Often eligible for depreciation under separate rules.

The lines between these can be blurry — a switchboard replacement that includes some compliance work, an LED retrofit that’s partly a fix and partly an upgrade. Your accountant’s judgement applies. What we can do is give you an itemised price that lets your accountant categorise each part of the job correctly.

Sole traders and home offices: what counts

If you run a registered business from your home, the business-use portion of electrical work that supports your work is generally claimable. A few common situations:

  • Dedicated home office circuit. A separate power circuit for your work setup so a kettle on the kitchen circuit doesn’t take out your computer mid-Zoom call. The portion attributable to business use is typically deductible.
  • Lighting upgrades in the work area. LED retrofits, additional downlights, task lighting over a desk. Same business-use portion rule.
  • EV chargers used for business travel. If you drive to client sites and log it as business kilometres, the business-use percentage of the install often becomes claimable.
  • Subsidiary buildings. A studio, workshop, or converted garage you run a business from — electrical work to that space is generally treated as business-use.

The standard ATO test is that the work has to genuinely support how you earn business income, and you need to apportion the personal vs business use honestly. Your accountant will walk you through it. We’ll happily itemise the work in a way that supports the apportionment.

The installed-and-operational-by-30-June rule

This is the part most people miss. To be deductible in FY26, work generally needs to be installed and operational before 30 June — not just paid for, not just ordered. Paying a deposit on 28 June for an install that happens on 4 July typically pushes the deduction into FY27.

What that means for booking right now:

  • Bigger jobs (switchboard upgrades, larger EV charger projects, multi-property bundles). Book early in June. Parts, scheduling and compliance paperwork all need time — the earlier you book, the more comfortably it fits before 30 June.
  • Quicker jobs (LED retrofits, surge protection, smoke alarms, single EV charger). More flexible — still bookable through June, just easier the earlier you give us a call.

Common questions about EOFY electrical work in Perth

Is electrical work tax-deductible on an investment property?+

Generally yes — but how you claim depends on the nature of the work. Repairs to keep the property in its current state are usually deductible in the year you pay for them. Capital works (improvements that add value or extend life, like a full switchboard upgrade or rewire) are typically claimed over time. Plant and equipment (like an EV charger) follow separate depreciation rules. Talk to your accountant about your individual situation before you book the work — we’ll give you the install timing and a phone price first.

Can a sole trader claim electrical work for a home office?+

If you have a dedicated work area at home and you run a business from it, you can typically claim the business-use portion of electrical work that supports that space. Dedicated power circuits, new outlets, lighting, and EV chargers used for business travel are common candidates. The percentage you can claim depends on how much of the home is used for business and your accountant’s assessment of the work’s purpose. ATO has clear guidelines on this — ask your accountant before booking.

What does the $20,000 instant asset write-off cover for FY26?+

The $20,000 instant asset write-off was made permanent in the May 2026 Federal Budget. It applies to eligible businesses purchasing assets costing less than $20,000 each, with no cap on the number of eligible assets. The asset must be first used or installed ready for use by 30 June to be deducted in this financial year. This is most relevant to commercial customers, but sole traders running a business from home may also be eligible. Confirm with your accountant whether your situation qualifies.

How early should I book a switchboard upgrade for EOFY?+

A switchboard upgrade is one of the bigger jobs in the EOFY mix — it involves parts, scheduling and compliance paperwork (Notice of Completion and Electrical Safety Certificate), so the earlier in June you book, the more comfortably it fits before 30 June. Smaller jobs — EV chargers, LED retrofits, surge protection, smoke alarms — have more flexibility but still benefit from booking early. Phone us with the address and we’ll be honest about what we can install in time.

Why isn't ordering the work before 30 June enough?+

For the work to be deductible in FY26, it generally needs to be installed and operational before 30 June — not just paid for or ordered. Paying a deposit on 28 June for an install that happens in July typically pushes the deduction into FY27. This matters most for bigger jobs — switchboard upgrades, three-phase work that needs Western Power involvement, and anything where parts have to be ordered in. Smaller work like LED retrofits, surge protection and smoke alarms has more flexibility, but it’s still safer to book early in June than leave it to the last week.

Do I need a licensed electrician for this work in WA?+

Yes. All 240V electrical work in Western Australia must be performed by a licensed electrician under the Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991. That covers switchboard upgrades, EV charger installs, lighting retrofits, surge protection, smoke alarm replacement and any work inside the meter box. Unlicensed work is illegal and voids your home or landlord insurance. We’re licensed EC009022 and issue a Notice of Completion and Electrical Safety Certificate on every job.
