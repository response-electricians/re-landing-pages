Power points Perth: when to upgrade, what modern outlets do, and how it works

Kitchen out of outlets? Cracked, yellowed, or does the plug fall out? Most people never think about their power points until one visibly fails. But modern outlets do far more than the old ones — USB-C charging built in, weatherproof outdoor versions, Cat6 data on the same faceplate. Here’s what to look for, what modern gives you, and how a whole-house refresh works.

The four signs your outlets need replacing

Power points don’t announce failure. They quietly wear out over 20-30 years while nobody looks at them. Here’s what to watch for:

1. Cracked or broken faceplates

Even hairline cracks. The faceplate isn’t just cosmetic — it seals the internal contacts from dust, moisture and finger contact. A cracked plate lets all three in.

2. Discolouration or yellowing

Older white plastic yellows with UV and heat over decades. That’s cosmetic. But browning specifically around the pin holes is different — that’s heat damage from arcing inside the socket. Yellow around a specific pin means that pin is running hot.

3. Loose plugs that fall out

The metal contacts inside a power point are designed to grip the pins of a plug firmly. Over years of insertion and removal, the metal fatigues and the grip weakens. A plug that falls out of its own weight is a socket at the end of its life.

4. Scorch marks or a burning smell

Any visible black stain, scorch mark, or slight burning smell around an outlet is a serious warning. It means an internal fault has been arcing under load. Stop using that outlet and book a replacement before anything else.

Aged homes note

If your outlets look like they’re from the 1970s or 80s (chunky flat plates, screws exposed, no earth pin holes even though your appliances have them), the whole set is due. Older outlets often lack modern safety features and their contacts are well past their reliable service life.

Why the kitchen is where this hits first

Most Perth kitchens were built with the appliance count of the era they were designed for. A 1990s kitchen assumed you had a toaster, a kettle, and maybe a food processor. A 2026 kitchen has a coffee machine, a kettle, a toaster, a stand mixer, a wireless charging pad, an air fryer, a rice cooker, and a phone charging on the bench.

The problem shows up in three ways:

  • Not enough outlets. Cords get unplugged and re-plugged constantly. Appliances migrate to power boards, which is fine electrically but ugly on the counter.
  • Everything on one circuit. Older kitchens have one power circuit for the whole room. The kettle plus the toaster plus the microwave draws close to the breaker limit — sometimes over it, and the RCD trips.
  • No USB. Every phone, tablet and watch charges through a plug-pack that hogs one whole outlet. Multiply by the number of devices in a family kitchen and you’ve lost half your sockets to chargers.

A kitchen refresh addresses all three: add more outlets, split heavy appliances onto dedicated circuits so they can’t trip each other, and fit USB-C outlets so chargers stop hogging real sockets.

Outdoor and alfresco: what IP66 actually means

Outdoor power points are a common Perth need — alfresco entertaining, poolside pumps, garden lighting, BBQ areas, hot tubs, outdoor Christmas lights. But not all outdoor outlets are equal. The IP (Ingress Protection) rating tells you exactly what an enclosure can survive:

  • IP44 — protected from splashes. Good for covered patios only. Not enough for genuine outdoor exposure.
  • IP54 — protected from splashes from any direction plus limited dust. Minimum acceptable for a genuinely outdoor location.
  • IP66 — dust-tight and protected from powerful water jets from any direction. This is what we install on all fully-exposed outdoor locations — pool areas, uncovered walls, hose-down areas.

The physical form of an IP66 outdoor GPO is distinctive: a grey enclosure with a hinged transparent cover that closes over the socket when nothing’s plugged in, and a rubber gland that seals around the cable when something is. Anything less than IP54 outdoors is a problem — a moment of rain into an unsealed socket is enough to trip the RCD at best, arc-fault at worst.

Cat6 data outlets: when wifi isn’t enough

Wifi handles casual browsing and streaming fine. But specific situations benefit from wired data:

  • Working from home on video calls — low, consistent latency means no dropped calls or freezes at critical moments.
  • Gaming — wired eliminates the millisecond variance that separates a win from a lag death.
  • 4K streaming to the main TV — wired handles the sustained throughput without buffering.
  • Home office setups with multiple devices — wired hub with wifi coming off it beats sharing wifi bandwidth between everything.
  • Thick internal walls or concrete floors — wired eliminates the black spots that wifi can’t reach.

The practical solution: fit combined faceplates with a 240V socket, USB-C ports and one or two Cat6 data outlets on the same plate at the desk, the media unit and any other high-demand location. No aesthetic downside — the plate looks identical to a normal outlet from a metre away.

Common bundling: what to combine with an outlet refresh

An outlet refresh often surfaces or pairs with other electrical work. The most common combinations:

  • Switchboard upgrade first. If the switchboard is at capacity or has old ceramic fuses, adding new outlets or circuits often pushes it over. Sensible to combine. See our switchboard upgrade guide.
  • Safety inspection reveals the problem. A whole-home safety inspection usually flags aged or damaged outlets before you notice them. The inspection report gives you a prioritised list with fixed prices.
  • EV charger install. If we’re running new consumer mains or upgrading the switchboard for an EV charger, that’s the ideal time to refresh outlets around the house.
  • Three-phase upgrade. Homes upgrading to three-phase power often refresh outlets at the same time — the switchboard is open anyway, and the circuit rebalancing is a natural chance to add dedicated appliance outlets.
  • Whole-house surge protection. A whole-house SPD at the switchboard protects the appliances plugged into your new outlets. Cheap add-on if we’re there for outlet work anyway.

Common questions about power points in Perth

How can I tell if a power point needs replacing?+

Look for four signs. First: cracked or broken faceplates — even hairline cracks let dust and moisture in. Second: discolouration or yellowing, especially browning around the pin holes — this is heat damage from arcing. Third: plugs that fall out or feel loose — the internal contacts have worn out and no longer grip. Fourth: scorch marks, black stains or a burning smell around the outlet. Any of these means the outlet has failed or is failing, and it should be replaced as a safety priority, not a cosmetic one.

Do USB-C power points charge faster than a normal charger?+

As fast, in most cases. Modern USB-C wall outlets typically deliver 30W or 45W per port, which matches or beats what a phone plug-pack charger provides. The bigger benefit isn't speed — it's convenience. Two USB-C ports live on the same faceplate as two 240V sockets, so charging cables run straight to the wall without a chunky plug-pack blocking an outlet. Every phone, tablet, watch, headphone case and portable device in the house charges from any USB-C outlet.

Can I replace power points myself in WA?+

No. Under WA's Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991, all electrical wiring and connection work — including replacing a power point — must be performed by a licensed electrician. Even if the faceplate looks like it just unscrews, the connections behind it are mains voltage. Unlicensed electrical work voids your home insurance and creates a hazard for the next resident. We're licensed (EC009022) and every job is certified with a Notice of Completion and Electrical Safety Certificate.

What does IP66 actually mean for an outdoor outlet?+

IP66 is the rating that says the enclosure is dust-tight and can withstand powerful water jets from any direction. The first number (6) is the dust rating — complete protection against dust ingress. The second (6) is the water rating — protection against heavy seas and high-pressure jets. For most Perth outdoor situations (rain, sprinklers, hose-down) IP66 is more than enough. Some poolside outlets use IP66 with a hinged transparent cover so they're usable in the rain without exposing the socket. Anything less than IP54 outdoors is a problem.

Do I need Cat6 data outlets if I have good wifi?+

For most casual use, wifi is fine. Cat6 wired data matters when you need consistent, low-latency performance: working from home on video calls, gaming, streaming to 4K TVs, running smart-home hubs, or when wifi struggles to reach a particular room (thick internal walls, concrete floors). One Cat6 outlet at the office desk and one at the media unit solves 90% of home network complaints. Modern faceplates combine a 240V socket, USB-C ports and Cat6 data outlets on the same plate, so you don't lose real estate on the wall.

Can you add power points without cutting the wall open?+

Sometimes, yes. If there's an existing outlet nearby, we can often extend from that point along a skirting or into a matching new position without opening the wall. Full new circuits (for kitchen appliances or a workshop, for example) usually need cavity access — but that's often minor and easily patched. We assess each situation on site and give you a fixed price that includes any patching required, so there's no argument about mess afterwards.

How long does a whole-house outlet refresh take?+

Typically half a day to a full day for a standard three or four bedroom Perth home, depending on how many outlets and whether we're adding new ones or just replacing existing. Straight replacement is quick — usually 15-20 minutes per outlet. Adding new outlets on new circuits takes longer, especially if there's cavity work involved. We isolate the affected circuits as we go so most of the house stays powered throughout.
