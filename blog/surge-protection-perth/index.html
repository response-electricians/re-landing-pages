Surge protection Perth: one switchboard install, every appliance protected

July is Perth’s wettest month of the year. August isn’t far behind. Lightning, voltage spikes, brown-outs — every storm season the unprotected gear loses. A whole-house surge protection device installs at the switchboard and protects every circuit in the property at the same time. Here’s how it works, what gets protected, and why a powerboard can’t do the same job.

Why Perth July storms drive surge damage

July is officially Perth’s wettest month of the year. Long-term BOM averages put it around 145mm of rainfall across 17 rain days, both the highest of any month. August follows close behind at around 122mm. Cold fronts and thunderstorms rolling in from the Indian Ocean bring three different things that damage electronics:

  • Direct lightning strikes — rare but devastating. A nearby strike (even a few hundred metres away) induces a huge voltage spike through the local network, with nothing to stop it reaching your appliances.
  • Switching transients — when Western Power re-energises a circuit after an outage, the inrush voltage can spike well above normal mains for a few milliseconds. That’s enough to fry sensitive electronics.
  • Voltage spikes from major appliance loads — aircon compressors, hot water systems and the neighbour’s welder can all create voltage spikes on the local feeder that propagate to your house.

Most surge damage you see isn’t from a direct lightning hit. It’s from one of those three sources, propagating through the network, with no SPD in the way to clamp the voltage before it reaches the appliance.

Why a switchboard SPD beats a powerboard

The most common confusion on this topic: surge-protected powerboards. They’re sold at every electrical retailer in Perth, often for $30 to $80, and the marketing implies they’ll save your gear in a storm. They will — but only what’s plugged into that specific board.

The fundamental difference:

Where the SPD sits What it protects
Surge-protected powerboard (at the appliance)Only what’s plugged into that one board — usually a TV, computer, or entertainment setup
Whole-house SPD (at the switchboard)Every circuit and every appliance in the entire property — oven, aircon, fridge, hot water, washing machine, EV charger, solar inverter, smart-home gear, plus everything plugged into every powerpoint

A powerboard is fine as a secondary layer of protection for the most sensitive gear — a high-end TV, a home server, a music studio. But it’s not a substitute for the switchboard install. The whole-house SPD intercepts the surge before it reaches any of the home’s circuits. The powerboard catches it at the appliance — which means everything else is already exposed.

If you’ve recently added solar, an EV charger, or significant smart-home gear, the case for whole-house protection gets even stronger. Those systems represent thousands of dollars of grid-connected electronics, and they sit on circuits a powerboard can’t cover.

What actually gets protected (and what doesn’t)

A whole-house SPD at the switchboard protects:

  • Every appliance hard-wired into the home — oven, cooktop, hot water system, aircon (including split-system control boards), heat-pump units, ducted aircon
  • Everything plugged into a power point in the home — TVs, computers, fridges, washing machines, the kettle, lamps, chargers
  • Smart and connected gear — smart fridges, smart hubs, security cameras, doorbells, NBN modem and routers
  • Solar inverters and EV charger electronics — the most expensive grid-connected systems in most modern homes
  • The lighting circuits — LED drivers can fail catastrophically on a surge

What it doesn’t protect:

  • Anything connected via phone or data line that bypasses your home electrical — e.g. a corded phone line directly into a modem (rare in 2026 but worth knowing)
  • Damage from a direct lightning strike on the home itself — this is a structural lightning protection question rather than a surge question. A Type 2 SPD reduces but cannot eliminate damage from a direct hit. For homes in high-risk areas, a Type 1 SPD or a dedicated lightning protection system is the conversation to have.

Type 2 vs Type 3 SPDs: when each makes sense

Australian Standard AS/NZS 1768 defines three categories of SPD. For homes, the two that matter are Type 2 and Type 3.

Type 2 SPD

Mounts inside the switchboard on a DIN rail. Handles most real-world surges — lightning-induced transients, network switching events, propagated voltage spikes. This is what we install as the primary whole-house protection on every job. A single Type 2 unit covers the entire property at the same time. We use Clipsal Type 2 SPDs sized to the switchboard.

Type 3 SPD

Smaller, installed close to specific sensitive equipment — behind a wall plate near a high-end TV, inside a server cabinet, near a music studio. Provides a fine-grained final layer of protection on top of the Type 2. Optional for most Perth homes, useful if you have specific high-value sensitive gear (home theatre, photography equipment, server, audio gear).

The standard recommendation: Type 2 at the switchboard for whole-house coverage, plus an optional Type 3 for the most valuable gear if you want belt-and-braces protection. Most Perth homes only need the Type 2.

For context: Type 1 SPDs

Type 1 SPDs are designed to handle direct lightning strikes on the building or supply, and are typically installed at the main intake on commercial buildings or properties with external lightning protection systems. For most Perth homes the Type 2 is the right call. We’ll let you know if your situation warrants Type 1.

Insurance: what an SPD does for your cover

Worth having the conversation with your insurer if you haven’t already. A few things to know:

  • Many home insurers offer discounts for homes with whole-house surge protection installed. Worth asking yours — it’s often around 5 to 10% off contents cover, sometimes more for high-value contents.
  • Some surge-related claims aren’t fully covered. Lightning damage is generally covered, but power surges from network switching may have lower limits or exclusions depending on the policy. Read the fine print.
  • Replacement isn’t the only cost. Even with full insurance, a multi-appliance claim after a storm involves excess, downtime, the hassle of proving the cause, and weeks waiting for replacements. The SPD avoids all of that.
  • Manufacturer warranties typically exclude surge damage. Your TV’s 5-year warranty almost certainly doesn’t cover lightning. An SPD bridges that gap.

2

On-site walkthrough

3

Power isolation + SPD install

We isolate the supply, mount the SPD on the DIN rail at the top of the board, terminate to the main bus bar, and connect to earth. No rewiring of your home’s circuits is needed — the SPD sits in parallel with the existing setup.

4

Test, commission, certify

Power restored, SPD tested to confirm it’s reading correctly (the status indicator on the front shows protection-active). Notice of Completion and Electrical Safety Certificate issued on the day. Lifetime workmanship warranty on the install.

Common questions about surge protection in Perth

What is whole-house surge protection?+

A whole-house surge protection device (SPD) is a small unit that mounts inside your switchboard and intercepts voltage spikes coming in from the grid before they reach your appliances. Unlike a powerboard with built-in surge protection (which only covers what's plugged into that one board), a whole-house SPD protects everything on every circuit downstream — TVs, computers, ovens, aircon, fridge, washing machine, EV charger, solar inverter. One device, every appliance.

Why is July the worst month for electrical surges in Perth?+

July is Perth's wettest month of the year — long-term BOM averages put it around 145mm of rainfall across 17 rain days, both the highest of any month. August is close behind. Cold fronts and thunderstorms rolling in from the Indian Ocean bring lightning, voltage spikes when the network re-energises after an outage, and brown-outs when load surges. Each of these is a different kind of surge, and unprotected electronics take the hit every time.

What's the difference between Type 2 and Type 3 surge protectors?+

Type 2 SPDs install at the switchboard and handle most real-world surges — lightning-induced transients, network switching events, voltage spikes from major appliances kicking on. That's what we install as the primary whole-house protection. Type 3 SPDs are smaller units installed close to specific sensitive equipment (a high-end TV, a home server, a music studio) — they provide a final layer of fine-grained protection on top of the Type 2. Most Perth homes only need the Type 2. We use Clipsal SPDs across both.

Does a powerboard with surge protection do the same thing?+

No. A surge-protected powerboard only protects what's plugged into that specific board — usually a TV, computer or entertainment setup. Everything else in the house (oven, aircon, fridge, washing machine, smart appliances, EV charger) is still exposed. A whole-house SPD at the switchboard protects every circuit in the property at the same time. Powerboards have a place as a secondary layer of protection for the most sensitive gear, but they're not a substitute for the switchboard install.

Will home insurance cover surge damage if I have an SPD installed?+

Coverage varies by insurer and policy — check yours. That said, many insurers view a properly installed whole-house SPD as a reasonable precaution and some offer discounts or improved coverage terms for homes with one fitted. More importantly, having an SPD means fewer claims to make: most surges that would have killed appliances simply don't reach them. Even with full insurance, replacement of multiple appliances after a storm involves excess, downtime and proving the cause.

Do I need a licensed electrician to install surge protection in WA?+

Yes. A whole-house SPD installs inside the switchboard and connects directly to the live supply — under WA's Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991, all switchboard work must be performed by a licensed electrician. We're licensed (EC009022) and issue a Notice of Completion and Electrical Safety Certificate on every install. Unlicensed work voids your home insurance and the SPD's manufacturer warranty.

How long does a surge protection install take?+

Typically a single visit, usually one to two hours on site for a standard install. The SPD mounts on a DIN rail inside your existing switchboard, terminates onto the main bus bar, and connects to earth. No rewiring of your circuits, no disruption beyond a short power-off while we make the connections. We test the device on commissioning to confirm it's reading correctly and walk you through the status indicator before we leave.
