Switchboard upgrade Perth: 5 signs it’s time and what to expect

Winter does the same thing every year in Perth. The heater pulls 2.4kW, the kettle pulls 2.2kW, the aircon pulls another 1.5kW, and the old switchboard trips. Ceramic-fuse boards from the 1970s and 80s weren’t built for modern household load — and 30%+ of Perth homes still run them. Here’s how to tell whether yours is overdue, what a modern install includes, and what to expect on the day.

Five signs your switchboard is overdue

Most Perth homeowners don’t think about the switchboard until it trips. By then it’s usually a Tuesday night in July, the kettle’s halfway to boiling, the fridge has been off for an hour, and you’re searching the meter box with a torch. Here are the five things to look for before that happens.

1

Ceramic fuses instead of safety switches

Round white porcelain holders with a copper wire inside. If your meter box has these, it pre-dates modern circuit breakers and almost certainly has no RCD safety switches at all. That’s the strongest single signal you’re due.

2

One RCD, or none at all

WA law requires at least two RCDs in every home. Many pre-2000 homes have one (added during a partial upgrade) or none (original 1970s/80s wiring). Either way, the rest of the circuits aren’t protected against electric shock.

3

It trips when high-load appliances run together

Heater on, kettle on, aircon on — and the lights go out. Modern boards handle simultaneous load. Old ones don’t. If you’ve learned to make tea before the heater goes on, your board is telling you something.

4

1970s or 80s board, never upgraded

If the home was built before 2000 and the meter box looks original, it’s well past its safe lifespan. Components degrade. Insulation breaks down. The wiring connecting it to your home is also likely the original. Worth a proper inspection.

5

Scorch marks, buzzing, melted plastic, or exposed wiring

Any visible damage is an emergency. Buzzing means an arc somewhere. Scorch marks mean past heat damage. Melted plastic means the board has been operating beyond its capacity. Don’t wait — phone us today.

If you saw yourself in any of these, the next question is what a modern install actually looks like. Here’s the before-and-after.

Why winter exposes old boards in Perth

Perth’s winter mornings are when old switchboards meet their match. The load profile from June to August is uniquely punishing for boards built in the 1970s and 80s.

A typical Perth winter morning, between 6:30am and 7:30am, looks like this:

  • Electric blanket running overnight on a low setting — around 300W
  • Reverse-cycle aircon kicks on at 6:30 in heating mode — 1,500W
  • Hot water system topping up the cylinder for morning showers — 2,000W
  • Kettle on for coffee — 2,200W
  • Heater in the bathroom or kitchen — 2,400W
  • Toaster, microwave, hair dryer, all over a 30-minute window

That’s peak load through a single switchboard that was originally sized for a 1970s household running a fridge, a TV, a couple of lamps, and maybe a small bar heater. Modern Perth households simply ask more of the board than the board was ever built to deliver. The first really cold morning — usually around mid-June — is when old boards start failing.

The fix isn’t to keep flipping the switch back. It’s to upgrade to a board that can handle the load.

What a modern switchboard install includes

The standard we install across every Perth residential switchboard upgrade is a Clipsal Resi MAX — the white metal enclosure most commonly seen on new builds and renovated homes. Inside it, the typical install includes:

  • Two RCD safety switches as the WA legal minimum — one protecting the power circuits, one protecting the lighting circuits. If something trips, only the affected half goes down.
  • Per-circuit RCBO protection (modern best practice) — a combined circuit breaker and RCD on every circuit, not just the two minimum. Costs a bit more but means a trip only takes down the one affected circuit. The fridge keeps running while you sort the bathroom fan.
  • Whole-house surge protection device (SPD) — mounted inside the board, intercepts voltage spikes from the grid or lightning storms before they reach your appliances. One device covers every TV, computer, oven and aircon in the house.
  • Clear circuit labelling — every breaker labelled with what it controls (kitchen power, lounge lights, hot water, AC, etc.) so future troubleshooting takes seconds, not minutes.
  • Physical capacity for future loads — we leave spare DIN-rail space and circuit positions for an EV charger, a ducted aircon system, a heat pump hot water unit, or a pool pump. You don’t want to be upgrading the board again in two years because you bought a Tesla.
  • Notice of Completion + Electrical Safety Certificate — issued on the day. Insurance covered. Lifetime workmanship warranty on the install.

For a deeper look at the safety-switch side specifically, see our Safety Switch Installation Perth page. For the EV-ready capacity angle, the Home EV Charging page covers what the board needs to support a charger now or later.

WA’s legal minimum — and where most homes fall short

Western Australia’s electrical regulations have required at least two RCDs in every home for years. One on the power circuits, one on the lighting circuits. Homes built or rewired after 2009 must have RCD protection on every single circuit — the more modern standard.

WA Regulation Note

All work inside a residential switchboard in Western Australia must be performed by a licensed electrician under the Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991. Installations require an Electrical Safety Certificate and Notice of Completion. WA law requires every home to have at least two RCDs — one for power, one for lighting. Licence number EC009022.

Where most pre-2000 homes fall short:

  • Zero RCDs. Original 1970s/80s wiring on ceramic fuses. Common in homes that have changed hands repeatedly without a board upgrade.
  • One RCD only. A partial upgrade in the 1990s or 2000s added one safety switch but left the rest of the circuits unprotected. Doesn’t meet the current minimum.
  • RCDs present but old. The mechanism degrades over time. RCDs more than 15 years old should be tested with calibrated equipment to confirm they still trip within the required time. Some don’t.

For the home-owner test you can do today, see our RCD testing in 10 seconds guide — the WA Government recommends pressing the T button on each RCD every three months.

What the install day looks like

Every switchboard upgrade follows the same five-step process. The whole job is usually done in a single visit.

1

Phone price + on-site walkthrough

You call us with a description of your meter box. We give a price estimate over the phone, then visit to confirm the scope on site. Final price locked in before any work begins.

2

Power isolation

On the day, we isolate the supply before opening up the board. For most properties this is at the main switch. For some older homes with degraded mains or unmetered supplies we coordinate isolation with Western Power.

3

Removal of the old board + termination of new

Out comes the old ceramic-fuse or partially-upgraded board. The new Clipsal Resi MAX enclosure mounts in the same location, every existing circuit is terminated cleanly onto its new RCD or RCBO, surge protection is installed at the top of the board, labels go on each circuit.

4

Testing every circuit

Every circuit is energised one at a time and tested with calibrated equipment. RCD trip times are verified, polarity checked, earth continuity confirmed. Any circuit that doesn’t pass is fault-found and fixed before we move on.

5

Certification + handover

Notice of Completion and Electrical Safety Certificate issued on the day. We walk you through the new board, show you how to do the quarterly press-button RCD test, and answer any questions. Lifetime workmanship warranty on the install.

When you also need consumer mains or underground power

Sometimes a switchboard upgrade reveals that the supply itself needs work. Two scenarios where this comes up.

Consumer mains

The consumer mains cable is the supply that runs from your meter to the switchboard. On older homes it can be undersized for modern load — especially if you’re adding an EV charger or ducted aircon. Or it can be physically degraded after decades in the sun, with cracked insulation or weathered terminations. Either condition means the cable itself needs replacing as part of the switchboard work. We size it for your current and likely future load so it doesn’t become the next bottleneck.

Underground power and Western Power coordination

If your property is in a suburb with underground power, or the supply itself is being upgraded (from single-phase to three-phase, or to a higher amperage), the work involves Western Power. We handle the application and coordinate the connection work on your behalf. It adds time to the overall job, so worth flagging early if you suspect it applies.

For larger jobs — full rewires, three-phase upgrades, or commercial switchboards — the principle is the same but the scale is different. See our main switchboard upgrades page for the service overview, or ring us and we’ll talk through what your specific situation needs.

Common questions about switchboard upgrades in Perth

How do I know if my switchboard needs upgrading in Perth?+

Five common signs: ceramic fuses instead of modern circuit breakers; only one (or no) RCD safety switch; the board trips when high-load appliances run together (heater, kettle, aircon); the board is from the 1970s or 80s and has never been upgraded; or there are visible signs of damage such as scorch marks, buzzing, melted plastic or exposed wiring. Any one of these is reason to get it looked at by a licensed electrician. Two or more, and an upgrade is almost certainly the right call.

What's the legal minimum for safety switches in WA?+

WA law requires at least two RCDs (residual current devices) in every home — one protecting the power circuits, one protecting the lighting circuits. Homes built or rewired after 2009 must have RCD protection on every circuit. Pre-2000 homes that have never been upgraded often have only one RCD, or none at all. Whatever you've got, the test button on each RCD should be pressed every three months to confirm it's working.

Why is winter the worst time for old switchboards in Perth?+

Winter morning routines stack high-load appliances together. The heater pulls around 2.4kW, a kettle pulls 2.2kW, an electric blanket overnight pulls another 0.3kW, the reverse-cycle aircon kicks on at 1.5kW. Old ceramic-fuse boards from the 1970s and 80s weren't designed for this load profile, and the first hard cold snap of the year exposes them. The fix isn't to keep flipping the switch back — it's to upgrade the board to one that can handle modern household load.

What does a modern switchboard install include?+

A modern Clipsal Resi MAX install includes the new white metal enclosure, two RCD safety switches as the legal minimum (or per-circuit RCBO protection as the modern best practice), individual circuit breakers for each circuit, whole-house surge protection at the board, clean circuit labelling for troubleshooting, and physical capacity for future loads like an EV charger, ducted aircon, heat pump or pool pump. On completion you receive a Notice of Completion and Electrical Safety Certificate, plus a lifetime workmanship warranty.

Do I need a licensed electrician to upgrade my switchboard?+

Yes. Switchboard work in Western Australia must be performed by a licensed electrician under the Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991. The board carries live mains voltage that can't be safely isolated by the home owner — even with the main switch off, the cables coming in from the meter are still live. Unlicensed switchboard work is illegal and voids your home insurance. A licensed install also issues a Notice of Completion and Electrical Safety Certificate, which your insurer (and any future buyer) will need.

How long does a switchboard upgrade take?+

Install day itself is half a day to a full day depending on the property — single-storey homes are faster, two-storey and larger family homes take longer. We do the work in a single visit where possible. The full process from booking to commissioned (parts, scheduling, compliance paperwork) varies with the job. Phone us with the address and a description of your meter box and we'll come back the same day with a realistic timeframe.

Will I need consumer mains or underground power work too?+

Sometimes. If your existing consumer mains cable (the supply from the street to your meter box) is undersized for modern loads or has degraded over time, it needs upgrading as part of the switchboard work. For underground power suburbs and properties where the supply itself is being changed, we coordinate the Western Power application as part of the job. Both add to the lead time, so worth flagging on the phone call if you suspect it applies to your property.
